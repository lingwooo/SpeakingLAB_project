01 · Research aim

What helps teenagers feel ready to speak?

This research explores four strategies for improving IELTS Speaking among teenagers: shadowing, AI speaking practice, speaking clubs and self-recording. It also examines the difficulties teenagers report, the ways they prefer to learn and their confidence when speaking English.

A closer look at speaking practice

The project brings together learner perspectives and an experience-based comparison of two practice formats.

02 · Research methodology

How the project explored the question

The research used a questionnaire to learn about teenagers’ experiences with IELTS Speaking, alongside a practical comparison of speaking clubs and AI speaking practice based on personal experience.

What this approach can describe

Participants’ reported challenges, preferences and confidence.

How to read the findings

The project does not report a controlled test of speaking improvement, so it cannot establish which strategy is most effective.

04 · Analysis

Preferences can guide the next research question.

The questionnaire highlights grammar, fluency, vocabulary and nervousness as areas participants experience as challenging. It also shows that speaking clubs and AI speaking practice stood out in their reported preferences.

These responses help identify what learners say they need and the practice options they prefer. The questionnaire describes participants’ views, while the comparison of clubs and AI practice reflects personal experience. A practical study could examine how each strategy affects speaking development.

Next stepFurther practical testing is needed to assess the effectiveness of these strategies.

05 · Four strategies

Different ways to practise speaking

Each strategy offers a different practice format. The descriptions explain what learners do; this project did not establish the relative effectiveness of the methods.

06 · Research conclusion

Speak

Confidence grows through opportunities to practise.

The questionnaire identified grammar, fluency and vocabulary challenges, as well as nervousness during IELTS Speaking. Speaking clubs and AI speaking practice were popular among the respondents.

The findings suggest that teenagers may benefit from more opportunities to practise speaking English and develop confidence. Further practical testing is needed to assess how these strategies support speaking development.

The study offers a useful snapshot of learners’ experiences—and a starting point for further research.
